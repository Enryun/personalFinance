# Lessons from Doz: growing product discovery through useful content

Reviewed November 11, 2026. This is a planning note for future, incremental improvements to James Thang’s app website. It does not authorize publishing articles, changing app claims, or deploying changes.

## What prompted this review

James reports that Doz’s author shared approximately **7,000 search clicks and nearly one million impressions**, and emphasized the importance of blogging. We have not independently verified those metrics, their date range, filters, or contributing pages.

At those approximate totals, click-through rate would be about **0.7%**. A monthly result and a lifetime result are very different benchmarks. Impressions, website visits, App Store visits, and downloads must be measured separately.

## What we observed on Doz

| Observation on the review date | Lesson for our website |
|---|---|
| The blog index lists 69 posts covering specific questions and workflows. | A product can reach people through the problems they search for before they know its name. |
| The tools index lists 29 browser-based calculators. | A useful interactive answer can become a search entry point and give visitors a reason to share a page. |
| A comparison hub links to individual competitor comparisons with sourced claims. | Help people make a decision with accurate, current evidence and clear tradeoffs. |
| The site offers eight language options. | Localized content can serve additional audiences when the language and product support match. |
| The homepage links to guides, tools, comparisons, support, and download actions. | Educational pages should connect naturally to the relevant product and next action. |

Sources: [Doz homepage](https://getdoz.app/), [blog](https://getdoz.app/blog), [tools](https://getdoz.app/tools), [comparisons](https://getdoz.app/compare).

These observations show Doz’s content strategy. They do **not** establish that its blog, tools, translations, QR card, or any specific feature caused the reported traffic. That requires its page/query performance data and publication history.

## The main lesson for us

Our landing-page revisions explain the apps more clearly and improve technical eligibility for discovery. The next opportunity is to answer useful questions beyond the app names, using our own working products as evidence.

Each article should solve a specific task, show an actual workflow, explain relevant limitations, and link to the product where it helps. Google recommends original, useful content supported by firsthand experience. It also states that Google AI search features use the same SEO foundations and require no special AI markup. Neither technical compliance nor publication guarantees indexing, rankings, or recommendations.

References: [Google’s people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [Google AI features and websites](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

## Step-by-step roadmap

### 1. Measure before expanding

- [ ] Review Search Console clicks, impressions, queries, pages, countries, and devices for a consistent date range.
- [ ] Separate branded searches from searches for tasks or product categories.
- [ ] Confirm indexing and selected canonicals for priority pages. A successful sitemap submission alone does not confirm indexing.
- [ ] Establish App Store outbound-click measurement, including QR-origin visits where feasible. Use supported App Store attribution to measure downloads separately.
- [ ] Use [the Vola visibility baseline](./vola-search-baseline-2026-11-10.md) as historical context, not a current ranking report.

### 2. Run a small Vola content pilot

- [ ] Research and select three distinct questions with a clear connection to Vola.
- [ ] Inspect existing walkthroughs and articles; improve a relevant existing page instead of creating a duplicate.
- [ ] Prepare real demonstrations and verify every feature, privacy, language, and OS claim.
- [ ] Publish the initial English guides with descriptive titles, readable headings, author attribution, and useful internal links.
- [ ] Ensure articles appear in initial HTML, navigation, and the sitemap; check mobile readability and image loading.

### 3. Observe and improve

- [ ] Check indexing after publication and review performance monthly using comparable periods.
- [ ] Record each article’s publication date, target question, impressions, clicks, CTR, App Store visits, and attributed downloads when available.
- [ ] Improve pages receiving relevant impressions but few clicks; check intent and position before assuming the title is the problem.
- [ ] Share demonstrations with relevant communities and reviewers, following community rules and disclosing authorship.
- [ ] Test a fixed set of neutral AI discovery questions in fresh conversations. Record sources and repeat observations; one answer is not a reliable visibility measurement.

### 4. Expand from evidence

- [ ] Translate useful, promising guides into supported languages with language review.
- [ ] Add sourced comparisons only after checking alternatives firsthand.
- [ ] Build a small tool only when it solves a real audience task and can be maintained.
- [ ] Extend the approach to another product when the pilot provides a useful workflow and evidence for the next investment.

## Editorial rules

- Prefer a few complete, original guides over a large batch of generic posts.
- Keep speech-to-text privacy separate from optional cloud AI processing in Vola copy.
- Keep Folio’s content English-first while the app supports English only.
- Use Vietnamese first for Ô Ăn Quan and emphasize space exploration and education for Galaxy Gallery.
- Do not invent search volume, test results, testimonials, accuracy claims, or competitor limitations.
- Date substantive updates honestly and maintain factual claims as the apps change.
- Keep product content organized around each app’s audience; do not mix unrelated topics into an article merely to mention more products.
